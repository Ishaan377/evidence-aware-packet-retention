How this was tested

The algorithm and every comparator were frozen on 2026-10-30 at 17:00:51, with hashes of 15 files, before any test capture was opened. Tuning used separate development cases only. The test covers 6 cases: five days of CSE-CIC-IDS2018 attacks and a WRCCDC capture of a human red team.

Suricata 7.0.3 with 52,778 ET Open rules scanned each complete capture and each reduced capture the same way. EBR received no alerts, labels or signatures. Attack windows and addresses come from the dataset publisher.

Disclosure. The first frozen run stopped before anything was scored, because one 4.3 GB capture was PCAPNG, which EBR does not read. It was converted to classic PCAP with a separate tool, and Scapy confirmed all 18,890,171 packets match in bytes, wire length and timestamp, with 0 mismatches. Its reference scan was redone from the converted file, and no frozen file changed.

Findings that did not favour EBR

An IDS with real rules keeps more alert instances at very low caps. At 1%, IDS-triggered capture keeps 91.7% of attack alert instances against EBR's 49.2%. That comparator needs a signature engine running at capture time, and its signature recall is capped by what those rules can see, but at the tightest caps it is the better choice.

The time-travel hypothesis was not supported. Rules published after the capture were expected to favour EBR. IDS-triggered capture still kept 92.9% of those alerts against EBR's 85.0% at 5%, because most new rules fire on flows that older rules had already flagged.

The human red-team capture is EBR's weakest case. On WRCCDC, EBR recovers 60.7% of attack signatures at 5% against 78.6% for IDS-triggered capture. 11 signatures are never recovered at any cap, and 10 of them are SMB lateral movement or SMB exploits. Later experiments found two causes: payload-free scan flows took most of the budget, and EBR treated SMB as opaque. Capping the scan flows mattered more.

More budget did not always mean more detection. 3 WRCCDC signatures present at a lower cap are absent at 10%. An early version of this report blamed gaps inside flows, and that was wrong: the affected login flows alert at every fidelity level when scanned alone. The loss went away once fewer scan flows were retained, which suggests retained scan traffic was interfering, though the exact mechanism inside the IDS was not established.

The attacker was not always named. In 2 labelled case(s), EBR's host ranking does not contain the attacker, because a command-and-control server or a very quiet attacker initiates little or nothing.

Reduction can create alerts. Across all cases and caps, EBR produced 96 attack alert instances absent from the complete capture, against 147 for the cutoff and 12 for IDS-triggered capture with fill. Most are rate rules firing on a different host pair.

What each component contributes

Each row removes one component and repeats the test. Scores are mean attack signature recall.

Diminishing returns does most of the work. Removing it, or removing all scoring, drops recall sharply. Pivoting and turn heads made no measurable difference to signature recall on this test, and rarity and automation changed it only slightly when removed one at a time, so this study does not show that those parts are needed. The ledger is a real trade: it uses a tenth of the budget, which costs some detection at the tightest caps in exchange for exact flow accounting.

Limits

Lossless gzip on these captures reaches 51.3% of the original size and loses nothing. EBR goes further by discarding data, so the two are not interchangeable.

Suricata cannot score attacks it does not detect on the complete capture, so those episodes count only through the ledger.

CSE-CIC-IDS2018 is a generated lab dataset, and its attackers are easy to separate. WRCCDC is more realistic but has no labels here.

A reduced capture cannot rebuild what was dropped. Bulk file contents and most encrypted payload are gone by design.

The test has few independent cases. A larger study across other sites is needed before claiming general superiority.

Repeating one complete capture reproduced 100.0% of alert instances, but a later check found that reduced captures vary more between scans, by up to 4.5 points of alert instance recall on one case, with no change in signature recall.
