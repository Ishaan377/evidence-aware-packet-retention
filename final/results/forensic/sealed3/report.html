What this means

The verification loop is the strongest result. Repair beat EBR alone at every cap by a clear margin, and it beat IDS-triggered capture at 2%, 5% and 10% with the same rule knowledge. At 1% that comparison is inconclusive.

EBR alone is not always ahead. On these scan-dominated captures it fell behind IDS-triggered capture at 1% and 2%, was level at 5%, and led at 10%. Earlier tests were kinder to it, so traffic type matters.

The scan cap helped only a few cases. It improved signature recall in 3 of 8 scored cases and lowered it in none. At 5% and 10%, the average improvement was not clear enough to rely on. It is best treated as protection against an occasional scan-heavy case.

Version 3 was frozen on 2026-10-31 at 00:54:12, with hashes of 22 files, before any of these captures was opened. The test results therefore reflect the version fixed in advance.

What repair cost

Repaired flows used 15.6%, 13.1%, 5.0% and 1.4% of the budget at the four caps. Repair made 3 of 64 case comparisons worse. 249 of 2041 usable alerts stayed lost at 5%.

Why the results came out this way

The cap had little to protect on IoT captures. A botnet host produces millions of scan flows but few flows with content, so that content usually fits with or without the cap.

EBR alone missed login sprays. On the Hide and Seek capture it lost 110 of 158 attack alert instances at a 10% cap, mostly telnet logins using known default passwords. Each attempt has the same size and shape, and the difference lies in the password, so diminishing returns treats them as repeats. This is a real blind spot in the idea that does most of EBR's work.

Repair struggles with rate-based rules. Most alerts still lost at 5% come from scan rules that need many flows close together, and restoring those can take more budget than exists.

Scan noise

Scanning identical selections twice changed 12 of 32 comparisons, by 0.4 points of alert instance recall on average and 6.1 at most.

A correction. Earlier reports said signature recall did not move between scans. Here it moved by 0.4 points on average and by 12.5 on one case with only 8 signatures. It is still the steadier measure, but small differences on small cases should not be read as real.

Limits

The WRCCDC 2018 slices are from the same event as a capture used in development, at different hours. They are unseen data, not a new network.

8 scored cases is a small test, and several come from a few malware families.

IoT-23 captures are the first 300 MB of longer recordings, and they are dominated by scanning from one infected host.

This report covers a rule-based detector only. It says nothing about what a human analyst could reconstruct.
